Song licensing

Most worship songs are copyrighted. If your church shows song lyrics on a screen or streams its services, you need the right license. Here is how to get one.

Why you need a license

Songwriters and publishers own the words and music of most modern worship songs. Putting the lyrics on a projector, printing them, or playing them in a livestream is a form of copying, so the law asks churches to have permission. A license gives that permission and makes sure songwriters are paid.

CCLI (most churches start here)

  • CCLI Copyright License lets you project and print lyrics for songs in their catalog during services.
  • CCLI Streaming License covers playing those songs in livestreams and posted service videos. Get it in addition to the Copyright License if you stream.

Visit ccli.com to see prices for your church size and sign up.

Other licenses

  • OneLicense covers many hymnals and Catholic and traditional publishers that CCLI may not. Visit onelicense.net.
  • CCS WorshipCast offers a streaming license that covers additional songs and publishers. Search for "CCS WorshipCast" to find them.

Not sure which you need? Check who owns the songs you sing most. The song credits or the license websites will tell you.

Public domain hymns

Older hymns whose copyright has expired, like many written before 1929, are in the public domain and need no license. Be careful: a newer arrangement or updated words of an old hymn may still be copyrighted.

Show your license number

When you add a song in Worship With Me, put your CCLI license number in the song credits (for example, "CCLI License #1234567"). It will appear with the song so your church stays in line with license rules.
